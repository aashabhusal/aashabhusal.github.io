Featured work

Research

Comparative Analysis of Capital Structure: Privately-Owned vs Government-Owned Banks

Nabil Bank vs Rastriya Banijya Bank · five fiscal years (FY 2077/78–2081/82), ten bank-years, audited figures only
Final-year BBS research · Supervised by Prof. Durga Datt Pathak

4 of 5 years the government bank was the more leveraged one
(10.22× vs 9.18×)
8.98% RBB's Tier 1 capital ratio by FY 2081/82
0.48 points above the NRB minimum
12–27% by which audited profit fell below the unaudited figures
every year, both banks

Three claims about private versus government banks in Nepal get repeated constantly and tested rarely — that the private bank is the more leveraged, that the state bank holds the thicker capital cushion, and that the private bank's higher returns are structural. Tested against five years of audited statements, none of the three survives. Rastriya Banijya Bank carried the higher debt-to-equity ratio in four of the five years, ending at 10.22× against Nabil's 9.18×. Its capital adequacy ratio fell 2.95 percentage points and crossed below Nabil's, with Tier 1 capital ending at 8.98% — barely half a point above the regulatory minimum. And the returns gap was not there at the start: ROE was effectively identical in FY 2077/78, and RBB out-earned Nabil the following year.

What the data does support is a difference in financing flexibility rather than in prudence. RBB, unable to raise equity from the market, funds itself from reserves and retained earnings — and when those earnings turned negative in FY 2080/81, its capital ratio had nowhere to go but down. Nabil's capital position and its profitability move independently (r = +0.40); RBB's move almost in lockstep (r = +0.94). The study was built from audited figures only, after audited profit proved 12–27% lower than the unaudited quarterly numbers in every single year.
